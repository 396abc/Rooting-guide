Overview

What this guide covers

Three parts. Part 1 is required. Parts 2 and 3 build on it independently.

Guide structure

1. Root — ADB, unlock bootloader, patch boot image, install Magisk and Zygisk
2. What you can do with root — ReLSposed Xposed framework, ReVanced for patching Spotify and YouTube
3. Google Wallet — Restore contactless payments and any banking apps blocked by root
✓ Everything in this guide is free and open source. Magisk, TrickyStore, ReLSposed, ReVanced, all of it has public source code on GitHub. Nothing shady, no random APKs from forums.
Before You Start

What rooting actually does to your phone

Worth reading before you start, especially the first two points.

Unlocking the bootloader wipes the phone completely. Android enforces this, there is no way around it. Back everything up first.
Over-the-air system updates will stop working. When a new OS version drops, you will need to flash it manually, which also wipes Magisk and means you will need to re-root.
Google Wallet and tap-to-pay stop working immediately after root. Part 3 of this guide fixes that, though it needs a quick re-run occasionally when Google updates its integrity checks.
Most banking apps keep working once you configure Magisk's DenyList. Stricter apps that still fail are also fixed by Part 3, which makes the device look clean to Google.
A handful of apps with strict DRM, like Netflix HD or Spotify offline, may lose access or drop to lower quality.
Unlocking the bootloader generally voids the manufacturer warranty.
Before You Start

Back up before you do anything

The bootloader unlock wipes the phone completely. Cover these bases first.

Photos and videos. Google Photos auto-sync is easiest, or just copy the DCIM folder to your PC.
Contacts. These should already be synced to your Google account, but open the Contacts app and check there is nothing sitting locally only.
App data. Settings → System → Backup covers most things. Apps without cloud sync, games, note apps, etc., you will lose progress on.
Two-factor auth codes. This is the one people forget. If you use Google Authenticator, export your accounts before wiping. Losing 2FA codes can lock you out of accounts with no recovery path.
Google Wallet cards. Make a note of what you have saved. You will re-add them manually after Part 3.
⚠ Once you run the unlock command the phone wipes immediately. No confirmation screen will save you if you did not back up.
Before You Start

What you will need

An Android phone on Android 14, 15, or 16 with an unlockable bootloader. Written and tested on the Nothing Phone (4a) Pro running NothingOS 4.1.
A Windows PC. The commands use PowerShell syntax. If you are on Mac or Linux, swap the .\ prefix for ./.
Android Platform Tools from Google. This is what gives you the adb and fastboot commands.
Google USB Driver. Windows needs this to recognise your phone when it is in fastboot mode.
The stock firmware ZIP for your phone, matching your exact current build. Step 3 explains how to find it.
Magisk APK. Grab the latest stable release from GitHub, not from third-party sites.
A USB cable that carries data, not just power. A lot of cheap cables are charge-only and will not work with ADB.
A completed backup. Seriously, see the section above.
Part 1 — Root with Magisk

Installing Magisk

Seven steps: get ADB working, unlock the bootloader, patch the boot image, flash it back, done.

1

Getting ADB working

Download Android Platform Tools from Google and unzip it somewhere easy to find, like C:\platform-tools. Inside you will find adb.exe and fastboot.exe, the two tools you will use throughout this guide.

Open PowerShell inside that folder. Quickest way: Shift + right-click anywhere inside the folder, then choose Open PowerShell window here. Because Windows does not put ADB in your system PATH automatically, you need to prefix every command with .\. So instead of typing adb devices, you type .\adb devices.

On your phone, go to Settings → About phone → Build number and tap it seven times. You will see a message saying developer mode is enabled. Head to Settings → Developer options and turn on both OEM Unlocking and USB Debugging. OEM Unlocking must be enabled before the bootloader unlock command will work.

Connect the phone via USB and run:

.\adb devices

Your phone will show a popup asking if you want to allow the connection. Tap Allow. The command should now print your device serial number. If it says unauthorized, hit Allow on the phone again.

2

Getting Windows to recognise fastboot

Send the phone into fastboot mode:

.\adb reboot bootloader

The screen goes minimal, usually black with white text or a logo. Run:

.\fastboot devices

If nothing shows up, Windows is missing the fastboot driver. This is normal and easy to fix.

⚠ Open Device Manager (Win + X → Device Manager). You will see an unrecognised device, probably under "Other devices". Right-click it → Update driver → Browse my computer for drivers → Let me pick from a list. Browse to the Google USB Driver folder, select android_winusb.inf, and choose Android Bootloader Interface from the list.

Run .\fastboot devices again. Your phone serial number should appear this time.

3

Downloading your phone's firmware

Magisk works by patching your phone's boot image and flashing it back. To do that, you need a copy of the stock firmware matching your exact current build. Grab the wrong version and you will bootloop.

Your build number is at Settings → About phone → Build number. Note it down exactly before downloading anything.

ℹ Once downloaded, verify the file: certutil -hashfile firmware.zip SHA256 in PowerShell. Compare the output to the checksum on the download page. If they match, the file is fine.

Extract the ZIP. You are looking for two files:

init_boot.img — what Magisk patches on Android 13 and newer devices
boot.img — older devices use this instead
ℹ Most phones running Android 13 or newer use init_boot.img. If you are not sure, check the XDA thread for your device.
4

Unlocking the bootloader

⚠ Stop here if you have not backed up. This wipes everything.

With the phone still in fastboot mode, run:

.\fastboot flashing unlock

Use the volume keys to select Unlock the bootloader and confirm with the power button. The phone wipes itself and reboots. From now on you will see a warning screen on every boot. That is just what an unlocked bootloader looks like, it is not a problem.

Go through the setup process again. You only need to get as far as having Wi-Fi and a working phone. Do not worry about restoring everything yet.

5

Patching the boot image

Copy the init_boot.img from your PC to the phone. Adjust the path on the left to wherever you extracted the firmware:

.\adb push C:\firmware\init_boot.img /storage/emulated/0/Download/init_boot.img

Install Magisk on the phone. Either download it directly in the phone's browser from the GitHub releases page, or push it via ADB:

.\adb install C:\Downloads\Magisk.apk

Open Magisk, tap Install next to the Magisk row, then Select and Patch a File. Open Downloads and select init_boot.img. Magisk will patch it and save the result to /storage/emulated/0/Download/ with a name like magisk_patched_[random].img. Pull it back to your PC:

.\adb pull /storage/emulated/0/Download/magisk_patched_*.img C:\platform-tools\

You should end up with something like magisk_patched_abc123.img sitting in C:\platform-tools.

ℹ If the pull command errors, open your phone's Files app and check Downloads. The file saves to /storage/emulated/0/Download/ and should be there.

Before flashing, quickly check you are on the right slot and that the partition exists:

.\fastboot getvar current-slot
.\fastboot getvar has-slot:init_boot

current-slot should say a. has-slot:init_boot should say yes. If it comes back as no, your device uses boot.img instead. Run the same process with boot.img and replace init_boot with boot in the flash command.

6

Flashing the patched image

Get back to fastboot if you are not already there:

.\adb reboot bootloader

Flash the patched file. The wildcard in the filename should match automatically:

.\fastboot flash init_boot C:\platform-tools\magisk_patched_*.img

If your device uses boot instead, change init_boot in that command accordingly. Then reboot:

.\fastboot reboot

Let the phone boot all the way into Android, then open Magisk. If the top row shows Installed with a version number, you are done.

✓ Magisk is installed and root is working. That is the hard part out of the way.
7

Turning on Zygisk

In Magisk settings (top right), toggle Zygisk on and reboot. Zygisk is what lets modules like ReLSposed and TrickyStore hook into the system. Both Part 2 and Part 3 need it.

ℹ Root is done. Head to Part 2 to see what you can actually do with it, or skip to Part 3 if getting Google Wallet back is the priority.
Part 2 — What You Can Do With Root

ReLSposed & ReVanced

Root on its own does not do much visibly. This is one of the more useful things you can do with it. ReLSposed is the Xposed framework: it lets modules reach inside running apps and change how they behave, without touching the APK. ReVanced is built on top of that and lets you patch apps like Spotify and YouTube to remove ads, unlock features, and so on. There are hundreds of other Xposed modules if you want to explore further.

1

Installing ReLSposed

The original LSPosed is no longer updated and does not work on Android 16. The maintained fork is JingMatrix/Vector, which goes up to Android 16 and is what you want here.

Download the latest stable ZIP from that page. In Magisk, go to Modules → Install from storage, select the ZIP, and reboot. After rebooting, install the manager APK from the same release. You will get a persistent notification in your status bar. Tap that to open the manager.

ℹ The project was renamed from LSPosed to Vector during a rewrite. If other guides refer to it as LSReposed or ReLSposed, they mean this. Stick to stable releases rather than debug or CI builds.
2

Patching Spotify with ReVanced

Install ReVanced Manager from GitHub. One thing to know upfront: ReVanced patches target a specific version of each app. The latest Spotify from the Play Store almost certainly will not match, so you will need an older build from APKMirror.

Open ReVanced Manager, select Spotify, and look at the patch notes to confirm the supported version. At the time of writing, v9.1.68.1874 was the working version.
Find that version on APKMirror. Pick the variant with a shield icon (APKMirror-signed), meaning it has not been modified since leaving the developer.
If you have a newer version of Spotify installed, uninstall it first. Then install the APKMirror version.
Back in ReVanced Manager, select Spotify, hit Patch, let it run, then install the result.
ℹ If something goes wrong, Magisk's log console will show you exactly what failed. Nine times out of ten it is a version mismatch. Double-check you are using the version ReVanced Manager is expecting.
Part 3 — Google Wallet

Getting Google Wallet working again

When you root, Google detects it through Play Integrity and marks the device as untrusted, which kills contactless payments and blocks some stricter banking apps. This section walks through making the device appear clean again so Wallet and everything else starts working.

⚠ Compatibility note: Tricky Addon Enhanced does not currently work on the Nothing Phone (4a) Pro and likely other Nothing phones as of August 2026. Stick to the standard Tricky Store Addon. If you experiment with alternative addons or layer multiple tools on top of each other without knowing exactly what each one does, you risk ending up with a broken integrity setup that requires a full firmware reflash to recover from. Follow these instructions as written before trying anything more advanced.
✓ This is not just a Wallet fix. Getting your device to pass Play Integrity at DEVICE or STRONG level sorts out any app that was blocking you due to root, banking apps, payment apps, certain streaming services. Anything that does a full integrity check gets fixed here.
⚠ One thing worth knowing: by spoofing integrity, you are telling Google your device is clean and unmodified. That is the point, and it is what makes everything work. It also means Google's own protections cannot flag anything that tries to exploit root. Be sensible about what you install.
ℹ If you run into issues with Part 3, the XDA thread by Arkl1ne has an active comments section with community solutions for a range of problems people have run into.

2

Setting up Magisk's DenyList

Open Magisk settings (top right icon) and make sure these are enabled:

Zygisk (should already be on from Part 1 Step 7)
Magisk Hide
Enforce DenyList

Go to Configure DenyList and add these apps. After adding each one, tap the app name to expand it and tick all the sub-processes underneath. There is a blue bar that runs across the top of each entry and it needs to go all the way to the right edge. If it is only partial, some of that app's processes are still visible to root checks.

Google Play Services
Google Wallet
Google Play Store
Google Services Framework. It appears in the list, but if it refuses to let you tick it that is fine, just skip it.
3

Installing the modules

In Magisk, go to Modules → Install from storage and flash these one by one:

Tricky Store
Tricky Store Addon (v4.4 or newer)
Anti-Bootloop, optional but recommended

No need to reboot between each one. Flash them all, then reboot once at the end.

ℹ If Anti-Bootloop is installed and you get stuck in a bootloop, the recovery combo is Vol− → Vol+ → Vol− → Vol− → Vol+. If that does not work, hold both volume buttons while powering on.
4

Reboot

Reboot the phone. The modules will not do anything until the first proper boot after flashing, so this step actually matters.

5

Configuring Tricky Store

Open KsuWebUI and tap into Tricky Store. Do these in order:

Menu → Select All, then deselect anything that does not need integrity spoofing, games, social media, that kind of thing.
Tap Save.
Menu → Keybox → Repository. Pick any entry shown in green. Green means it has not been revoked by Google yet.
Menu → Set Security Patch → Get Security Patch Date → Save. In newer versions of the addon (v5.0+) this option is called Set Default Policy instead.
⚠ Red entries are revoked and will not work. If everything is red, just wait a few days since new keyboxes show up regularly. Keyboxes get patched by Google roughly every 2 to 3 weeks. When Wallet stops working, just re-run step 5 to pick a fresh green keybox and you will be back up.
6

Checking integrity

Open the Play Integrity checker app and run a check. You are looking for one of these:

MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY ✓ MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY — acceptable

DEVICE_INTEGRITY is enough for Wallet in most places. BASIC_INTEGRITY only means something went wrong. Go back to step 5 and try a different keybox.

7

Getting Wallet to activate

Google caches integrity results on its servers, so even with everything configured correctly Wallet might not activate immediately. Usually it sorts itself out within 24 hours. If you can wait, just leave it.

If you need it working now, you can force a refresh by clearing app data. Here is what that affects:

⚠ Clearing this data signs out every Google account on the device, so have your passwords ready. It also wipes Maps Timeline, removes all Wallet cards, and resets some Play Store settings. Everything comes back once you sign in again.

If you are okay with that: Settings → Apps, then clear storage for each of these:

AppAction
Google WalletClear storage once
Google Play ServicesClear storage twice
Google Play StoreClear storage once
Google Services FrameworkClear storage once
✓

Re-add your cards

Wallet should be working now. Open it and add your cards back. Physical bank cards usually need a minute to verify and will send you a code via text. Virtual cards show up almost immediately.

ℹ If Wallet breaks again after a Google update, it is almost always just a patched keybox. Just open KsuWebUI, go to Tricky Store, and pick a new green keybox. This happens roughly every 2 to 3 weeks. Should not take more than a couple of minutes.
